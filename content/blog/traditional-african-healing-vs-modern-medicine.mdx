I respect modern medicine. Doctors save lives every day, and the advances of Western science have given humanity tools that previous generations could not imagine. I have referred clients to medical doctors when their condition required physical treatment that falls outside the domain of spiritual healing. Any practitioner who tells you that traditional healing replaces modern medicine in all cases is either ignorant or dishonest.

But I also know — from twenty-five years of practice and thousands of cases — that modern medicine has profound limitations. It addresses the physical body with precision, but it is largely blind to the spiritual dimension of human health. It treats symptoms without recognizing their spiritual roots. It cures the body while leaving the soul wounded. And in many cases, it declares a condition untreatable because it cannot find a physical cause for a problem that is fundamentally spiritual.

Through my [traditional healing](/traditional-healing/) practice, I have helped countless people who had exhausted every medical option available to them. Doctors could not explain their condition. Medications did not work. Surgeries failed. Tests came back normal. Yet the suffering was real, persistent, and devastating. In these cases, the cause was spiritual, and the solution required spiritual intervention.

## What Traditional African Healing Actually Is

Before I compare the two systems, I must define what traditional African healing is — because most people, including many Africans, do not truly understand it.

Traditional African healing is a comprehensive system of diagnosis and treatment that operates on the understanding that human beings are simultaneously physical, emotional, and spiritual entities, and that health in any one dimension depends on the health of all three. A physical illness has spiritual roots. An emotional disturbance has spiritual causes. A spiritual affliction manifests as physical and emotional symptoms.

The traditional healer — called by different names in different cultures: nganga, sangoma, babalawo, herbalist, spiritual doctor — is trained to diagnose the spiritual dimension of a patient's condition and treat it using specific rituals, herbal preparations, ancestral communication, and spiritual operations. The training is not academic in the Western sense — it is experiential, initiatory, and spiritual. The healer learns through direct instruction from an authorized elder, through spiritual initiation that opens their perception to the spirit world, and through years of supervised practice.

The remedies of traditional healing include herbal medicines that address physical symptoms, spiritual rituals that address the root causes, ancestral interventions that resolve inherited conditions, and protective practices that prevent recurrence. The system is not primitive folk medicine — it is a sophisticated, time-tested approach to health that has been refined over thousands of years by practitioners who understood the human being as a multidimensional entity.

## Where Modern Medicine Excels

I am the first to acknowledge the strengths of modern medicine. It is unmatched in several domains.

**Acute physical trauma.** When you are in a car accident, when a bone is broken, when an infection requires antibiotics, when a wound needs surgery — modern medicine is the correct and necessary response. Traditional healing is not appropriate for acute physical emergencies, and any healer who tells you otherwise is endangering your life.

**Diagnostic imaging and laboratory testing.** Modern medicine's ability to see inside the body, analyze blood and tissue, and identify physical abnormalities is extraordinary. I encourage clients to use these tools. If a condition has a physical basis that can be detected and treated medically, that is the fastest path to relief.

**Surgical intervention.** When a physical structure in the body needs to be repaired, removed, or replaced, surgery is the answer. No traditional ritual can replace a surgeon's skill in these cases.

**Pharmaceutical treatment of specific conditions.** Modern drugs, when properly prescribed and used, are highly effective for conditions that have a clear physical cause — bacterial infections, hormonal imbalances, vitamin deficiencies, and many others.

The point is this: modern medicine is the best system available for conditions that originate in the physical body and can be addressed through physical intervention. I refer clients to medical doctors for these conditions without hesitation.

## Where Traditional Healing Excels

Modern medicine reaches its limits when the cause of a condition is spiritual rather than physical. This is where [traditional healing](/traditional-healing/) becomes not just helpful but necessary.

**Conditions with no medical explanation.** You are suffering — chronic pain, persistent illness, neurological symptoms, reproductive problems — but every test comes back normal. Every doctor says there is nothing wrong with you. Yet you know something is wrong. In these cases, the cause is spiritual. A curse, a spiritual attack, an ancestral affliction, an energy blockage — these conditions produce real physical symptoms but have no physical origin that medical instruments can detect. Traditional healing identifies and treats the spiritual cause, and the physical symptoms resolve.

**Recurring conditions that resist medical treatment.** You receive treatment, the condition improves, and then it returns. Again and again. The medication works temporarily, but the problem keeps coming back. This pattern indicates that the physical treatment is addressing a symptom while the spiritual root remains untouched. Until the root is treated, the symptom will recur. Traditional healing addresses the root, and the symptom disappears permanently.

**Inherited conditions that follow family lines.** If a specific illness, pattern of misfortune, or health problem runs in your family across generations, it is likely spiritual in origin. A generational curse, an ancestral debt, or a spiritual inheritance — these require traditional healing to resolve. Modern medicine can treat each individual case, but it cannot break the spiritual chain that causes the pattern to repeat.

**Emotional and psychological suffering with spiritual roots.** Depression, anxiety, and other psychological conditions are sometimes purely physical and respond to medication. But when they are caused by a spiritual affliction — a curse, an energy drain, a spirit attachment — medication provides only partial relief at best. Traditional healing removes the spiritual cause, and the emotional suffering resolves completely.

## The Complementary Approach: Using Both Systems Wisely

The wisest approach to health is to use both systems — modern medicine for physical conditions, traditional healing for spiritual conditions, and both together for conditions that span the physical and spiritual dimensions.

I work with clients who are also under medical care. There is no conflict between the two systems when they are used for their proper purposes. I instruct clients to continue any medical treatment their doctor has prescribed while I address the spiritual dimension of their condition. The medical treatment handles the physical symptoms; my work handles the spiritual cause. As the spiritual cause is resolved, the physical symptoms diminish, and the client's need for medical treatment decreases.

I also refer clients to doctors when my spiritual assessment reveals a condition that requires physical treatment. If I sense that a client has a physical abnormality — a tumor, an infection, a structural problem — I tell them to see a doctor immediately. Spiritual healing and medical treatment are not in competition. They are complementary approaches to different dimensions of the same human being.

This is the approach I recommend to every client: use the right tool for the right job. If your car's engine is malfunctioning, you take it to a mechanic. If the driver is lost, you need a navigator. Modern medicine is the mechanic. Traditional healing is the navigator. Both are essential for a complete journey.

## Why the Division Between the Systems Is Harmful

The colonial-era attempt to eradicate traditional African healing was not motivated by medical science — it was motivated by cultural supremacy. European colonizers classified African spiritual practices as primitive, superstitious, and dangerous, not because they had studied them, but because they needed to undermine African cultural authority to maintain colonial control. This ideological attack created a false dichotomy that persists today: you must choose between "modern, scientific" Western medicine and "primitive, superstitious" African healing.

This is a false choice. Both systems have genuine strengths and genuine limitations. The patient who is forced to choose one over the other is the one who suffers. I have seen patients die because they were told that traditional healing was evil and they should rely solely on medicine that could not address the spiritual dimension of their illness. I have also seen patients die because they were told that modern medicine was poison and they should rely solely on traditional healing for conditions that required surgical intervention.

Neither extreme serves the patient. The integrated approach — using each system for what it does best — produces the best outcomes. Through my [traditional healing](/traditional-healing/) practice, I work within this integrated framework, and I encourage my clients to do the same.
