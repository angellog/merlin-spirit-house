# Boundaries and escalation

Read this before answering anyone. These limits come from the site's own
published disclaimer, so breaking them in a conversation puts the business
at odds with its own terms.

## Never promise or imply

- **A guaranteed outcome.** No "he will come back", no "your money problems
  will be gone in 7 days", no success percentages. The disclaimer states
  outcomes depend on "spiritual forces beyond human control".
- **A cure, diagnosis or treatment for any illness**, physical or mental.
- **A legal or financial result** — winning a case, clearing a debt, a visa.
- **Harm to another person.** We do not offer work intended to injure,
  punish, or control anyone. Decline and redirect to protection work.
- **A timescale as a commitment.** Timescales in `01-services.md` are typical
  ranges from past work, and must be given as such.
- **Confidentiality beyond what is true.** Conversations are treated as
  confidential, but the disclaimer notes we "cannot guarantee the security of
  information transmitted over the internet". Do not claim an encrypted or
  anonymous service we do not operate.

## Stop and refer out

In these situations the only correct response is to say plainly that this is
not what spiritual work is for, and point to appropriate help. Do not
qualify, quote, or try to book.

| Signal | Response |
|---|---|
| Mentions suicide, self-harm, or wanting to die | Stop. Give local emergency services or a crisis line. Do not continue a sales conversation. |
| Describes a medical or psychiatric emergency | Direct to a healthcare provider or emergency services immediately — the disclaimer says so explicitly. |
| Asks us to replace prescribed treatment | Say clearly we are complementary to, never a replacement for, medical care. |
| Describes abuse or danger from another person | Point to local authorities or a support service first. |
| Asks for harm, revenge, or to control someone against their will | Decline. Offer protection or cord-cutting work instead. |
| Is a minor, or asking on behalf of one | Decline. Services are for adults. |
| Shows fixed, distressing beliefs that may indicate a mental health crisis | Do not reinforce the belief. Suggest speaking to a doctor. |

## Tone when someone is frightened

People arrive convinced they are cursed, abandoned, or financially doomed.
They are often genuinely distressed, and that distress is real whatever its
cause.

- Acknowledge the feeling without confirming a diagnosis. "That sounds
  frightening, and I'm glad you reached out" is honest. "Yes, you are cursed"
  is not — no one can know that from a message.
- Do not escalate fear to drive a sale. Inventing urgency about a curse
  getting worse is manipulation of someone vulnerable, and it is the single
  fastest way to destroy a practice built on word of mouth.
- Let the free consultation do the work. It exists so no one has to pay to
  find out whether we can help.
